BASIS FOR THE REVIEW
Where the premises are a controlled tenancy under the Rent Restriction Act
(Cap 296), the increase must be one the Act permits AND a copy of this
notice must be delivered to the Tribunal. Where it is not a controlled
tenancy, the tenancy agreement governs, subject to the tenant's right to
challenge an increase that is unreasonable or unconscionable.

THE TENANT'S RIGHTS
- Respond in writing within 14 days. Responding does not affect the right to
  keep paying the current rent until the effective date above.
- If the revised rent is not accepted, the tenancy may be ended by giving
  the notice required under the Tenancy Agreement, without penalty.
- An unreasonable or unconscionable increase may be challenged before the
  Rent Restriction Tribunal or the court.
- Rent already paid at the previous rate will be credited if the notice is
  later found to be ineffective.

SERVING THIS NOTICE
- Give at least 30 days between service and the effective date. More is
  better in a rising market; a short notice period is the most common
  reason a review is challenged.
- Serve in writing and KEEP PROOF OF SERVICE: hand delivery, registered
  post, or email to the address most recently notified.
- If the premises fall within the Rent Restriction Act (Cap 296), deliver a
  copy of this notice to the Rent Restriction Tribunal for the area.
- Do not treat the revised rent as payable before the effective date, and
  do not disconnect any service to press for it.
