This is a drafting aid, not legal advice. Have a Kenyan advocate review

THE CORRECT LAW. Kenyan residential tenancies are governed by the Rent
Restriction Act (Cap 296), the Law of Contract Act (Cap 23), the Distress
for Rent Act (Cap 293) and the Land Act. The Landlord and Tenant (Shops,
Hotels and Catering Establishments) Act (Cap 301) applies to shops, hotels
and catering premises — NOT to a home.

2.1 The tenancy runs for the term above.
2.2 If the Tenant remains in occupation after expiry with the Landlord's
    consent, the tenancy becomes periodic on the same period as the rent
    then being paid, and either party may end it by one period's written
    notice (Land Act, s.57).

3. RENT
Monthly rent: KES ____________   Due: 1st of each calendar month
Payable to: ______________________________________
3.1 Paid in full and in advance on or before the due date.
3.2 Payment is treated as made on the date funds are RECEIVED, not the date
    the Tenant initiates them. A receipt is issued for every payment.
3.3 If rent is unpaid 14 days after the due date, the Landlord may recover
    the actual cost of financing the shortfall, supported by a record.
    IMPORTANT: this is not a penalty. Flat late fees have been struck down
    by Kenyan tribunals as unlawful and punitive — see Tribunal Reference
    E153 of 2023. Do not replace this clause with a flat percentage.

4. SECURITY DEPOSIT
Deposit paid: KES ____________
4.1 Held as security, NOT as advance rent. It does not discharge the first
    month's rent.
4.2 No interest accrues in the Landlord's favour.
4.3 Permitted deductions only: (a) rent properly due and unpaid; (b) utility
    charges outstanding and evidenced; (c) damage beyond fair wear and tear,
    proven against the move-in condition report.
4.4 Balance refunded within 30 days of vacating, with a written itemised
    statement of deductions and supporting evidence.
4.5 Fair wear and tear — faded paint, worn flooring, minor scuffing from
    ordinary use — is not chargeable.
Note: Kenyan law sets no statutory maximum. One to two months' rent is
normal practice; a much larger deposit risks being held unconscionable.

5. UTILITIES AND SERVICES
5.1 Tenant pays: electricity, water, gas, internet, refuse — own consumption.
5.2 Landlord pays: property rates, and any service levy agreed in writing.
5.3 The Landlord shall NOT disconnect or withhold water, electricity,
    lighting or refuse to force payment or a departure. Depriving a tenant
    of a service without the Tribunal's leave is a criminal offence.
5.4 No tampering with utility meters.

6. REPAIRS
6.1 Landlord keeps in repair: structure, roof, walls, ceiling, floor,
    drainage, gutters, and main water, electricity and sanitation services.
6.2 Tenant keeps clean and sanitary: interior, garden, parking. Responsible
    for minor repairs, bulbs, consumables, and their own blockages.
6.3 Tenant reports any defect in writing within 48 hours. A claim for a
    defect not reported in that window may be resisted.
6.4 Health or safety risk — burst pipe, live cable, failed lock — report
    immediately by phone as well as in writing.

7. LANDLORD'S UNDERTAKINGS
7.1 No entry without consent, except agreed scheduled access or a genuine
    emergency (fire, flooding, risk to life). Otherwise 24 hours' written
    notice.
7.2 The Tenant is entitled to quiet enjoyment. No harassment, intimidation
    or repeated contact intended to force a departure or a higher rent.
7.3 No works that unreasonably disturb occupation; reasonable notice first.

8. TENANT'S UNDERTAKINGS
8.1 Residential use only. No trade, no animals, no subletting or assignment
    without the Landlord's prior written consent.
8.2 No nuisance to neighbours; no breach of law.
8.3 Reasonable care of the premises; no damage beyond ordinary use.

9. INSURANCE
Tenant advised to carry personal cover; Landlord advised to keep buildings
and contents cover for the term.

10. RENT REVIEW
10.1 Rent is fixed for the term. Review once in any 12 months, on not less
     than 30 days' written notice, and not mid-term unless allowed here.
10.2 Where the premises are a controlled tenancy under Cap 296, the increase
     is not due or recoverable until valid written notice of intention has
     been served. Otherwise this Agreement governs, subject to the right to
     challenge an increase that is unreasonable or unconscionable.
10.3 A tenant who does not accept the increase may end the tenancy on
     notice under clause 12 without penalty.

11. DEFAULT
11.1 Written notice first, specifying the breach, with a reasonable time to
     remedy. Only then any further step.
11.2 Possession ONLY by order of the Rent Restriction Tribunal or the
     court, executed by a bailiff. No changing locks, no removing the
     tenant or their goods without such an order.
11.3 Distress for rent, where ordered, only by a LICENSED AUCTIONEER under
     Cap 293 — never by the landlord, agent or caretaker.

12. TERMINATION
12.1 A periodic tenancy ends on at least one full calendar month's written
     notice, expiring at the end of a tenancy month.
12.2 Notices must be in writing, delivered by hand, registered post or
     email to the address most recently notified.
12.3 On termination: vacate, return all keys, leave the premises as found
     fair wear and tear excepted, and settle amounts lawfully due.
12.4 A tenant ending a fixed term early does so at their own cost, unless
     the Landlord consents in writing.

13. DISPUTES
Amicable resolution in writing first, then the Rent Restriction Tribunal for
the area, or the Small Claims Court within its jurisdiction.

14. PERSONAL DATA
14.1 The Landlord processes the Tenant's ID number, name, phone and payment
     records to administer this tenancy, issue receipts and keep proof.
14.2 Kept only as long as necessary, secured against unauthorised access,
     not disclosed except as required by law or to enforce this Agreement.
14.3 The Tenant may ask what is held, correct it, or have it deleted or
     anonymised once unnecessary, under the Data Protection Act, 2019.

15. GENERAL
Entire agreement. No change valid unless in writing and signed. If a clause
is unenforceable the rest continues. Governed by Kenyan law.
